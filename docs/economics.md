# Pilot unit economics

Record actual quotes and transactions; never substitute a retail price for wholesale cost. Evaluate each site separately and include operator time even if the founder performs it.

## Per-unit contribution

`net unit contribution = sale price - sales tax borne by operator - landed product cost - payment fee - site commission - expected refunds/shrink`

Payment fees may have a fixed component; low-price singles can become unattractive. Apply the actual merchant fee schedule. If tax is collected on top of shelf price, account for it separately; verify local tax treatment.

## Monthly site contribution

`monthly contribution = sum(units sold × net unit contribution) - machine/software/connectivity - electricity if billed - scheduled service labor and travel - repairs/insurance allocation - other site costs`

Track cash flow separately: machine purchase, installation, initial stock, deposits, and working capital occur before steady sales. Estimate payback only from cash flow and state assumptions. Model low/base/high sales, a stockout, a machine outage, and a product mix shift. A deal that only works with optimistic traffic should not pass the purchase gate.
